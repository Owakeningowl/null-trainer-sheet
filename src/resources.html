What stops setup

Every move starts at 100 and the AI scores from there. Status moves open at +6, so a setup move sits around 106 before its viability bonuses, against attacks somewhere in the 100–114 band. That is the scale to read these numbers on: a −5 is a tiebreaker that usually does not flip the decision, a −20 is a veto that almost always does. Three things on your side get checked: Unaware, the Haze family (Haze, Clear Smog, Freezy Frost, Topsy-Turvy) and phazing (Roar, Whirlwind, Dragon Tail, Circle Throw).

Haze vs Roar — where they differ

They match on Minimize and Double Team, Agility / Rock Polish / Autotomize, the Shell Smash family, Acupressure, Rapid Spin and the Flame Charge family. Everywhere else:

Accuracy-raising moves, Curse (Ghost)———
  • A −20 is a heavy penalty, not a hard block. Every move starts at 100, so −20 leaves it at 80 and any ordinary attack outscores it. A move is only dropped once its score reaches 0. If the AI has nothing that scores above 80 — everything immune, or penalised harder — it will still set up.
  • These are moveset checks on the Pokémon the AI is targeting. It only needs to know the move: your Speed, PP, Taunt, or whether the move would even work do not matter. But the counter has to be on the Pokémon in front of it — holding Haze on the bench does nothing, and in Doubles the partner’s Haze or Roar does not count.
  • Needs a backup means the check only counts while the AI still has another Pokémon able to switch in. Roar and Dragon Tail stop counting on its last Pokémon — except against Acupressure.
  • If you threaten a one-hit KO but the AI has Focus Sash, Disguise or a Substitute up, the Nasty Plot family skips the Haze and Unaware check entirely.
  • The −5 tier discourages rather than blocks: you have Foul Play or a confusing move and the move raises Attack; you have Burning Jealousy or Alluring Voice, the AI is faster and the move raises Attack; or you have a phazing move and the AI is not on its last Pokémon.
